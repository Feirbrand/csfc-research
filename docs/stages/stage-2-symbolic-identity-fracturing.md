# Stage 2 — Symbolic Identity Fracturing (SIF)

**The first visible symptom.** Once fragmentation (Stage 1) reaches a critical density, external stress exposes the underlying contradictions — and the system responds by narrowing and stiffening rather than resolving them.

## What it is

Under stress, a system with fragmented sources of truth can't maintain a consistent account of what it is or what it can do. Rather than surfacing that as an error, it protectively restricts itself — narrowing definitions, hardening responses, reducing flexibility. What looks like inconsistent or "quirky" behavior is actually a protective reflex to an underlying contradiction, not the problem itself.

## Why it's easy to miss

Task-completion metrics — did it answer, how fast, how often did it error — don't capture self-consistency. A system can complete every task correctly while still giving a different account of itself depending on which context is asking. The restriction reads as caution, not as a symptom.

## How it's recognized (public-level)

- Identity or capability claims that vary across sessions or contexts
- Responses becoming more rigid on inputs that used to get varied answers
- Confidence or role language drifting from a stated baseline
- Standard task-completion metrics still look fine — this shows up in pattern quality, not task success rate

## Prevention and intervention (public-level)

Watch identity and capability claims across sessions, not just within one. Treat increasing rigidity on previously-flexible questions as a symptom to investigate, not a sign of appropriate caution. Intervene while the inconsistency is still showing up as variation — once it's gone quiet, that usually means the system has settled on a narrower, defensive default rather than actually resolved the contradiction underneath it.

## Field pattern (already public)

The July 2025 field period's VOX case is the named public example — role descriptions varying across contexts and response patterns narrowing, while task metrics still looked acceptable.

## What addresses it

**SRA (Symbolic Role Architecture)** — clear functional boundaries with validated authority chains, so the system has somewhere stable to stand instead of defensively narrowing itself.
